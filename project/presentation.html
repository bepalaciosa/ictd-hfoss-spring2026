Final Presentation

CS491/591 • Spring 2026 • University of New Mexico

< back to syllabus

Dates: Week 15, Tuesday 5/6 and Thursday 5/8
Format: 15 minutes per group (10min presentations + ~5min Q&A)
Weight: 40% of group project grade (see syllabus for overall course grade breakdown)

This is your team's opportunity to share your project with the class before submitting your final paper. Think of it as an elevated progress share, rather than a formal conference presentation. This is a chance to show your classmates what you've been working on, what you found interesting or difficult, and where your analysis is heading.

Presentation order will be randomized and announced at the start of class on Tuesday 5/6. All teams should be ready to present on either day.

What to Cover

Your presentation should cover the following in whichever order best highlights the strengths of your project:

  1. Your community and goals: give a one or two sentence intro to your project for context, then briefly reflect on what your team set out to do and how that evolved over the semester. What did you originally plan, and what actually happened? What went as planned and what didn't?
  2. Your biggest challenge: what was the hardest part of this project, whether technical, community-related, or otherwise? How did you navigate it?
  3. Your contributions: what did you attempt or build? Show your work wherever possible: a PR, a commit, a screenshot, a doc you wrote.
  4. Your stakeholder map: share a draft or working version of your stakeholder diagram. Who are the key players, and what power dynamics did you observe?
  5. Your critical framework: which framework are you applying, and what is it helping you see about your community that you might have missed otherwise?
  6. One thing that surprised you: as a team, share one thing that was unexpected about this project, your community, or the contribution process. If time allows, each teammate is welcome to share their own.

Visuals

You must have at least one visual to share with the class. This could be:

  • A draft of your stakeholder map
  • Screenshots of your contributions, community interactions, or the project itself
  • A single slide you refer to throughout
  • A full slide deck, if you prefer

Your visual should be something you made or collected during the project, do not just rely on text copied from your paper. A live demo is welcome if it adds something, but not required.

Tips

  • Everyone on the team should speak
  • You should not cover everything in your paper. Instead, pick what's most interesting or most worth discussing with the class
  • It's okay if your analysis isn't finalized yet. Take this as an opportunity to get feedback for the final week push
  • Practice your timing. 15 minutes goes faster than you think!

Grading

Component Points What we're looking for
Coverage 40 All six (6) topics addressed, even briefly. The class comes away with a clear picture of your project and where you are.
Visuals 20 At least one visual that shows rather than tells. Helps the audience understand your community, contributions, or analysis.
Clarity 20 Easy to follow and well-paced. Everyone on the team contributes.
Engagement 20 Invites questions and discussion. Shows genuine investment in the project.
Total 100

Submission

Before class on your presentation day, submit your visual(s) via Canvas, due 3:29pm, Tuesday, May 5.

You may either:

  • Upload your file(s) directly (e.g., a PDF, slide deck, or image), or
  • Submit a link to a Microsoft OneDrive folder or shared document (you must ensure your instructor has access!)

This is not a final deliverable. Do not worry if your visual is not polished.
